## Performance

> Manual old-vs-new benchmark of the `--columnar` engine over the set in [`columnar_wip/benchmark-networks.md`](columnar_wip/benchmark-networks.md). This is **not** the CI `perf-pr.yml` check, which only sees the default OO path since the new core is flag-gated.

Single-threaded (`MODE=release OPENMP=0`), `--seed 123`. Codelength in bits. **`instr` is instructions retired** (`/usr/bin/time -l`). `time` is `--timing-json`'s `timing.total_s`.
- `-N10` rows run once (deterministic; `instr` carries the comparison).
- `-N1` rows are the interleaved minimum of 3.
- Driver: [`columnar_wip/bench-sync.py`](columnar_wip/bench-sync.py) `--carry-oo`, writing [`columnar_wip/pref-linkless-ab-results.tsv`](columnar_wip/pref-linkless-ab-results.tsv), old and new on every row of #1417's snapshot.
- Re-timed: the outliers in seconds, with [`columnar_wip/retime-outliers.py`](columnar_wip/retime-outliers.py), writing [`columnar_wip/pref-linkless-retime.tsv`](columnar_wip/pref-linkless-retime.tsv), as the minimum of 5, interleaved. No codelength changes, so `retime-changed.py` has nothing to run.
- The change itself ([below](#the-24-preferred-modules-runs)): the 24 preferred-modules runs, [`columnar_wip/bench-pref-runs.py`](columnar_wip/bench-pref-runs.py) writing [`columnar_wip/pref-linkless-vs-1417.tsv`](columnar_wip/pref-linkless-vs-1417.tsv), minimum of 3 interleaved, codelengths at full precision; OO from #1408's session, [`columnar_wip/pref-top-vs-oo.tsv`](columnar_wip/pref-top-vs-oo.tsv).
- Tables: [`columnar_wip/assemble-hier-gaps.py`](columnar_wip/assemble-hier-gaps.py) builds every table from the TSV that [`columnar_wip/tables-1042.py`](columnar_wip/tables-1042.py) `assembly` writes, including the OO cells. The REG table comes from `tables-1042.py reg`.

> **This PR offers a unit without links the modules of the next five units in the sweep under `--preferred-number-of-modules`, so the bias spends its merges where OO does (#1407, stacked on #1417).** #1417 offered such a unit, which after aggregation is a whole connected component, only the module of the previous lone unit, while K > K_pref. Those units paired once per aggregation pass, the large component's linked units took the rest of the merges, and netscience K = 50 `-2` squeezed its 379-node component into 5 modules: 5.137 bits against OO's 4.799. Now the unit is offered the modules of the next 5 units in the shuffled sweep, pairs or groups, which is OO's five random moves without a draw. It stays in the sweep, so it can re-sort into a cheaper group, and it gets the candidates in every move loop the bias steers, also at K = K_pref, where only the base codelength prices a re-balancing move. Only a move loop with the bias compiles the candidate in (`moveLoopWith`'s `LinklessPartners`), as before. The finding is F86.
>
> What the snapshot shows:
> - **On the 24 preferred-modules runs, 6 change** ([below](#the-24-preferred-modules-runs)). netscience K = 50 `-2`: 5.137 → 4.841 bits (−5.77%; +0.87% above OO, from +7.04%), at +3.0 ms (0.0115 → 0.0145 s). air30k K = 20 `-2` −0.36% and standard −0.08% in bits, at +3.2% and +1.1% in instructions. science2001 K = 25 `-2` −0.07%, polblogs K = 5 `-2` −0.16%, both at no cost in instructions. The other 18 have the same codelength.
> - **Worse cells:** netscience K = 50 standard has the same codelength at +13.7% in instructions (+3.4 ms), and polblogs K = 5 standard is **+0.008%** in bits at +3.7% in instructions, [explained below](#every-cell-that-is-worse-explained).
> - **No configuration of the snapshot changes codelength:** the only one with the bias, word_assoc (pref.), gives the same codelength, as it does in the 24 runs.
> - **Nothing else pays for it:** instructions +0.001% summed over the 332 configurations, median −0.005% over the 295 above 0.3G (5th–95th percentile −0.26% to +0.15%). Seconds +0.86% summed; the 17 outliers re-time to −1.3% to +1.7% at −0.06% to +0.18% in instructions.
>
> Columnar arms measured in one session, 2026-11-11, interleaved per row, with no other benchmark or test suite running.
> - **Old:** #1417's head `9e089e1b`, the base of this stacked PR, md5 `4ade69dbb25c7479ae8819c8e5889295`. #1408 landed as `3cb04006` with the same tree as its head, so once #1417 lands unchanged, the tip of `columnar-hierarchical-core` builds to this binary.
> - **New:** `56cb7f09`, md5 `2d03dc51d6dd080cf84913da767cce7b`.
> - **OO arms** are carried from #1417's TSV, which carried them from #1408's: this PR changes only columnar code.
> - **The feature build** is not re-run: none of its configurations sets `--preferred-number-of-modules`, so they all run the move loop without the candidate, which is the code they ran before. The C++ tests pass with OpenMP on and off and in the feature build.

- **netscience** has 268 connected components (1461 linked nodes; the largest 379, and 102 pairs). This PR keeps the large component in 11 modules (2 also holding small components) and the small components in 39 modules of up to 18 components; OO keeps 15 and 35 of up to 20; #1417 kept 5 and 45 of up to 8.
- **The gap left to OO under `-2`** (+0.87%) is in the range of OO's coarse-tune: OO's own aggregation ends at 4.904 bits at `-N1`, its coarse-tune takes it to 4.814, and the columnar `-2` search has no step that splits a module and re-sorts the pieces against the bias (F86).
- **The standard search lands on netscience's unbiased codelength** (3.364651, as under #1417): grouping whole components under one top module costs nothing in a hierarchy, because no flow enters their submodules.
- **The 18 runs with the same codelength** move −0.5% to +0.1% in instructions (netscience standard aside), and −2.7% to +7.2% in seconds on runs of 0.004 s to 5.8 s; the largest, word_assoc K = 3000 `-2` at +7.2%, retires 0.04% fewer instructions.

#### Every cell that is worse, explained

- **In bits, against #1417:** polblogs K = 5 standard, 7.592927 → 7.593534 (+0.008%), at +3.7% in instructions and +4.3% in seconds. polblogs has a few units without links, and the candidate changes the trajectory: the whole run visits such units 38 times (counted with temporary prints), so the instructions are a different search path, not the candidate's work. The same run under `-2` is −0.16% at +0.1%.
- **In seconds, on changed runs or runs with the bias:**
  - netscience K = 50 `-2` +26% (0.0115 → 0.0145 s, +25% in instructions), for −5.77% in bits. The units without links stay in the sweep and each visit evaluates 5 candidates.
  - netscience K = 50 standard +10.8% (0.0336 → 0.0373 s, +13.7% in instructions) for the same codelength: the hierarchical search runs the bias in its crossing and top regroups, whose units are mostly whole components, and every grouping of them is free there, so the extra visits buy nothing. Keeping the units in the sweep only while K > K_pref saved about 1% of this run's instructions at the same bits, and keeping the units in the sweep is what closes the `-2` gap (4.967 → 4.882 bits without the K = K_pref candidates, F86), so the cost stays.
  - air30k K = 20 `-2` +3.2% in instructions (+1.9% in seconds) for −0.36% in bits, and standard +1.1% (+3.4%) for −0.08%; its 6 small components are units without links.
- **In bits, against OO:** netscience K = 50 `-2` +0.87%, from +7.04% (above). Unchanged from #1417: word_assoc K = 3000 +0.80%; air30k is not comparable (F84).
- **In seconds, on the snapshot's bit-identical rows:** none after re-timing beyond noise. The 17 outliers (+10% to +89% in the sweep) re-time to −1.3% to +1.7% at −0.06% to +0.18% in instructions.

  search is worse.

  planted partition is worse than one-level: it is no reference, and the block's deltas are ~~struck~~.
- The plain search rows are the same runs as the `-C`, `-C -2` and `-C -N1` rows of that network.
- Every row is bit-identical: no family run sets a preferred number of modules.

higher-order input the engines deliberately optimize different objectives (F39, #1012). The columnar
engine adds the metadata term to the full higher-order objective; OO scores state nodes only, without
the physical-node codebook. Scoring each engine's `-2` partition under the other engine confirms it:
columnar's prices at 7.416 bits under `-C` and 11.092 under OO, and OO's at 9.119 and 9.225. Without
`--meta-data` the two engines agree on both partitions to every printed digit. So the row shows no delta,
